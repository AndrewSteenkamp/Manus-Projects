# Seed Saving and Stockpiling for Scarcity Preparedness

## Critical Seed Storage Principles

### The Four Essential Conditions

**1. COOL** - Consistent cool temperatures (ideally 32-41°F / 0-5°C)
**2. DARK** - Complete darkness to prevent premature germination
**3. DRY** - Relative humidity 40% or lower
**4. PROTECTED** - Safe from pests, rodents, and insects

## Proper Seed Drying Before Storage

### The Snap/Shatter Test
- **NOT READY:** Seeds bend, squish, or smash = still too wet
- **READY:** Seeds snap cleanly or shatter = properly dried

### Drying Methods

**Method 1: Screen Drying (Best)**
- Spread seeds thinly on mesh screen
- Air reaches both top and bottom
- Use fan for faster drying
- 1-2 weeks depending on seed type

**Method 2: Paper Plate/Coffee Filter**
- Spread seeds thinly
- Turn daily
- 1-2 weeks
- Less efficient than screen

**Method 3: Hanging Blossom Bags**
- Hang seeds in breathable bags
- Cool, dry, dark location
- Good for small quantities

**Critical:** Relative humidity must be 40% or lower during drying

## Long-Term Storage Containers

### Best Options (Ranked)

**1. Glass Jars with Airtight Lids**
- Mason jars with new lids
- Completely moisture-proof
- Pest-proof
- Can see contents
- Reusable indefinitely

**2. Metal Tins/Containers**
- Airtight seal essential
- Protects from light
- Pest-proof
- Long-lasting

**3. Mylar Bags with Oxygen Absorbers**
- Industry standard for long-term storage
- Heat-seal for airtight closure
- Add oxygen absorber packet
- Can achieve 10-20 year storage
- Blocks all light

**4. Plastic Pill Bottles**
- Good for small quantities
- Airtight
- Portable
- Free if you save medication bottles

**5. Ziploc Bags (Inside Larger Container)**
- Not airtight alone
- Must be inside jar or tin
- Good for organization

### "Open Storage" (Only in Ideal Conditions)
- Paper envelopes
- Mesh bags
- Breathable containers
- **ONLY IF:** Cool, dry (40% RH), dark, year-round stable conditions
- Allows seed respiration
- Good if unsure of seed moisture content

## Optimal Storage Locations

### Best (Ranked)

**1. Climate-Controlled Seed Vault**
- Dedicated refrigerator/freezer
- 32-41°F (0-5°C)
- 40% RH or lower
- Dark
- **Can achieve 10-20+ year storage**

**2. Basement/Cellar**
- Cool year-round
- Stable temperature
- Low humidity
- Dark
- North side of house

**3. Refrigerator**
- Must be dry (40% RH or lower)
- Seeds must be completely dry before storing
- Risk of frost damage if any moisture remains
- Use airtight containers

**4. Freezer (Advanced)**
- Seeds must be BONE DRY
- Any moisture = frost damage = dead seeds
- Can achieve longest storage (20+ years)
- Airtight containers essential

### Worst (AVOID)

**❌ Garage** - Temperature fluctuations kill seeds
**❌ Shed** - Humidity fluctuations kill seeds
**❌ Car** - Extreme temperatures
**❌ Kitchen** - Too warm and humid
**❌ Direct sunlight** - Triggers germination response

## Seed Saving Techniques (Become Seed-Independent)

### Easy Seeds to Save (Start Here)

**1. Peas and Beans**
- Let pods dry on plant
- Harvest when brown and crispy
- Shell out seeds
- Dry for 1 week
- Store

**2. Tomatoes**
- Scoop out seeds with gel
- Ferment in water for 3-5 days
- Rinse and dry thoroughly
- Store

**3. Lettuce**
- Let plant bolt (go to seed)
- Collect seed heads when dry
- Shake out seeds
- Store

**4. Radish**
- Let plant bolt
- Seed pods form
- Harvest when dry and brown
- Shell out seeds

### Advanced Seed Saving

**Squash, Pumpkin, Cucumber:**
- Let fruit fully mature (even overripe)
- Scoop out seeds
- Rinse and dry thoroughly
- Store

**Brassicas (Broccoli, Cabbage, Kale):**
- Let plant bolt and flower
- Seed pods form
- Harvest when dry
- Shell out seeds

**Peppers:**
- Let fruit fully ripen (change color)
- Cut open and remove seeds
- Dry for 1-2 weeks
- Store

## Creating Your Seed Bank

### Storage System

**Option 1: The Refrigerator Method**
1. Large airtight container (plastic bin with gasket seal)
2. Individual seeds in small glass jars or envelopes
3. Label everything (variety, date saved, source)
4. Silica gel packets to absorb moisture
5. Store in refrigerator (not freezer unless seeds are BONE DRY)

**Option 2: The Mylar Method**
1. Seeds in small paper envelopes (labeled)
2. Multiple envelopes in one Mylar bag
3. Add oxygen absorber
4. Heat-seal Mylar bag
5. Store in cool, dark location
6. Can achieve 10-20 year storage

**Option 3: The Mason Jar Method**
1. Seeds in small paper envelopes
2. Multiple envelopes in one mason jar
3. Add silica gel packet
4. Seal with new lid
5. Store in cool, dark basement/cellar

### Labeling System (CRITICAL)

**Every container must have:**
- Variety name
- Date saved/purchased
- Source (company or your garden)
- Germination rate (if tested)
- Expected viability date

**Example Label:**
```
Broccoli Calabrese
Saved: March 2025
Source: My Garden (3rd generation)
Germ Rate: 95% (tested Jan 2026)
Viable Until: 2029-2030
```

## Germination Testing

**Test stored seeds annually:**
1. Take 10 seeds from storage
2. Place on wet paper towel
3. Keep moist and warm
4. Count how many germinate in 7-10 days
5. 8+ = excellent (80%+)
6. 5-7 = good (50-70%)
7. Below 5 = replace seeds
