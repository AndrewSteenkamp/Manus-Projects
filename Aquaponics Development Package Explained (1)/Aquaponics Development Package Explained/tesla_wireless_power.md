# Tesla Research: Wireless Power Transmission and Free Energy Concepts

## Early Wireless Power Concepts (1890s)

### Theoretical Foundation
- From 1890s through 1906, Tesla spent significant time and fortune on wireless power transmission projects
- **Key Claim (1893)**: Told onlookers he could "conduct intelligible signals or perhaps even power to any distance without the use of wires" by conducting through the Earth
- Believed radio waves were worthless for power transmission since they would "diminish over distance just like any other radiation"
- Worked on idea that he might conduct electricity long distance through the Earth or atmosphere

### Early Experiments (1890-1893)
- Experimented with transmitting power by inductive and capacitive coupling using high AC voltages with Tesla coil
- Attempted wireless lighting system based on near-field inductive and capacitive coupling
- Public demonstrations lighting Geissler tubes and incandescent light bulbs wirelessly
- Spent most of decade working on variations but none succeeded commercially

## Colorado Springs Experiments (1899-1900)

### The Experimental Station
- Set up experimental station at high altitude in Colorado Springs during 1899
- Could safely operate much larger coils than in New York lab
- El Paso Electric Light Company supplied alternating current free of charge
- Funded by convincing John Jacob Astor IV to invest $100,000 ($3,779,600 in today's dollars)

### The Magnifying Transmitter
- Experimented with large coil operating in megavolts range
- Produced artificial lightning (thunder) with millions of volts and discharges up to 135 feet (41 m) in length
- At one point, inadvertently burned out the generator in El Paso, causing power outage
- **Crucial Discovery**: Observations of electronic noise from lightning strikes led him to conclude he could use "the entire globe of the Earth to conduct electrical energy"

### Claims of Extraterrestrial Communication
- Observed unusual signals from receiver which he speculated to be communications from another planet
- Reported to Red Cross Society in December 1900
- Expanded on signals in February 1901 Collier's Weekly article "Talking With Planets"
- Said signals could have come from Mars, Venus, or other planets
- Claims treated as sensational story by reporters

### The Century Magazine Article (1900)
- Tesla had agreement to produce article on findings for The Century Magazine
- Article titled "The Problem of Increasing Human Energy" appeared in June 1900
- Explained superiority of wireless system he envisioned
- Article was more philosophical treatise than scientific description

## Wardenclyffe Tower Project (1901-1906)

### Funding and Initial Plans
- Made rounds in New York trying to find investors for wireless transmission system
- **March 1901**: Obtained $150,000 ($5,669,400 in today's dollars) from J.P. Morgan
- In return for 51% share of any generated wireless patents
- Began planning Wardenclyffe Tower facility in Shoreham, New York, 100 miles east of NYC

### Expanded Ambitions (1901-1902)
- By July 1901, expanded plans to build more powerful transmitter to leap ahead of Marconi's radio system
- Tesla thought Marconi's system was copy of his own
- **December 1901**: Marconi transmitted letter 'S' from England to Newfoundland, defeating Tesla in race for first transatlantic transmission
- **June 1902**: Tesla moved lab operations from Houston Street to Wardenclyffe

### Project Failure and Financial Collapse
- **Investor Abandonment**: Investors on Wall Street put money into Marconi's system instead
- **Media Criticism**: Some in press began calling Tesla's project a hoax
- **Project Halt (1905)**: Project came to halt, perhaps due to Tesla's nervous breakdown in 1906
- **Financial Ruin**: Tesla mortgaged Wardenclyffe property to cover debts at Waldorf-Astoria ($20,000 = $627,800 today)
- **Continued Attempts**: After Wardenclyffe closed, Tesla continued writing to Morgan and his son Jack for further funding
- **Bankruptcy**: By 1915, Tesla was effectively bankrupt, most patents had run out

## Key Theoretical Claims

### Earth as Electrical Conductor
- Believed the entire Earth could be used as an electrical conductor
- Theorized that electrical energy could be transmitted through the Earth's natural electrical charge
- Claimed this would allow wireless power transmission to any distance

### "Invisible Light" Theory
- Believed radio waves were "invisible light" that would diminish over distance
- Thought his system would be superior because it used Earth's conductivity rather than radiation

### Global Wireless Power Distribution
- Envisioned worldwide wireless electric power distribution system
- Believed his magnifying transmitter could provide power globally
- Claimed system would be more efficient than traditional wire-based power distribution

## Suppression and Opposition

### Financial Opposition
- Wall Street investors chose Marconi's radio system over Tesla's wireless power
- J.P. Morgan withdrew support when project scope expanded beyond original agreement
- Media began portraying project as impractical or fraudulent

### Technical Challenges
- No feasible way existed at the time to wirelessly transmit large amounts of power over long distances
- Tesla's theories about Earth conduction were not scientifically validated
- Project required enormous amounts of capital with uncertain returns

### Competition from Radio
- Marconi's success with wireless telegraphy made Tesla's more ambitious power transmission seem unnecessary
- Radio communication proved commercially viable while wireless power transmission did not
