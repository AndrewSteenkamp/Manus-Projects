# Siener Van Rensburg: Comprehensive Research Study

## Basic Biographical Information

**Full Name:** Nicolaas Pieter Johannes ("Niklaas" or "Siener") Janse van Rensburg  
**Birth:** 3 August 1864, Palmietfontein farm, Potchefstroom district (now Tlokwe/Potchefstroom), South African Republic  
**Death:** 11 March 1926 (aged 61), Rietkuil farm, Ottosdal, North West Province  
**Nationality:** Transvaaler (South African Republic), later South African citizen  
**Known for:** Prophet of the Boers, Seer  
**Spouse:** Anna Sophia Kruger  
**Children:** 10 children including Willem Jacobus, Anna Sophia (Badenhorst), Anna Catharina, Maria Elizabeth  

## Historical Context and Early Life

Van Rensburg was born on the farm Palmietfontein in the Potchefstroom district during the era of the South African Republic (Transvaal Republic). He was the son of Willem Jacobus Janse van Rensburg and Anna Catharina Janse van Rensburg.

**Education and Early Development:**
- Received only 20 days of formal school training at the Rooipoort farm school
- Spent much of his youth as a cattle herder
- Could never write but assisted by his mother, learned to read from the Bible
- Never read anything else besides the Bible

**Early Signs of Prophetic Ability:**
- His mother commented on his visual hallucinations as a toddler
- These early visions seemed to disturb him
- General Hertzog later described him as "someone continuously distracted by a maze of imagery and symbolism"

## Role During the Boer War (1899-1902)

Van Rensburg played a significant role during the Second Anglo-Boer War, not as a combatant but as a visionary advisor.

**Military Service:**
- Van Rensburg and his brother Pieter were commandeered to participate in the war under General Sarel P. du Toit
- However, van Rensburg remained unarmed and never fired a shot
- His contribution was "a stream of visions and prophecies for the duration of the war"

**Key Relationships:**
- Became a trusted companion and advisor to **General Koos de la Rey**
- Also worked with **President Steyn**
- The devoutly religious de la Rey seemed to have considered him a prophet of God
- The extent of his influence with these figures is disputed by historians

**Specific Wartime Visions and Activities:**

1. **Vision of Defeat:** Early in the war, van Rensburg believed a nightly vision spoke to him only a day before the outbreak of war, with a message that his work was connected to God.

2. **Siege of Kimberley:** Following a vision, van Rensburg was beset with fear that would not dissipate. When this disturbed state continued into their sojourn in Kimberley, his superiors sent him home. He believed a new vision had revealed to him the defeat and loss of life that the war would bring about.

3. **Escape Predictions:** At Graspan, his disturbed state lifted permanently as a soldier was wounded on his side. According to van Rensburg, another vision had forewarned that van Rensburg then travelled with general Piet Cronjé but escaped the encirclement by British forces at Paardeberg.

4. **Family Rescue Vision:** In January 1901, van Rensburg had a vision indicating the flight of three Boer women, who were soon found and rescued by his host Willem Bosman. Days after rejoining De la Rey's commando, he had visions of members of his own family being captured and asked for leave to assist them.

## The Visions: Core Characteristics

**Total Number:** 700 visions have been documented

**Nature of Visions:**
- Van Rensburg interpreted his hallucinations as visions
- Usually connected to the welfare of the Boere, the Netherlands, and Germany
- Typically wrapped in a patriotic, religious format
- Have been interpreted by believers as predictions of future events

**Symbolic Interpretation System:**
- Van Rensburg developed a complex system for interpreting symbolic elements in his visions
- For example: a vision of the sisal plant was interpreted as a portent of an important meeting, assembly, or parliament
- He did not interpret all of his visions
- Some have been posthumously applied to more recent events as prophecies

**Documentation:**
- Many visions were recorded by his daughter Anna Badenhorst
- Some visions were recorded by reverend Dr Rossouw
- The collections of visions were considered inflammatory during World War II
- Distribution was prohibited and some copies were seized on orders of prime minister Jan Smuts
- Upon Anna's death, handwritten documents were transferred to Lichtenburg museum's archives, where they were rediscovered in 1991

## Documented Predictions and Their Interpretations

**Local Events:**
- Death of General Koos de la Rey
- Political transition of South Africa

**International Events:**
- Start of World War I
- Rise of Communism

**Specific Wartime Predictions:**
- Before the Battle of Tweebosch, van Rensburg gave predictions about how the enemy would approach along the Harts River
- He predicted when the enemy would be most vulnerable
- He envisaged how the victory would enhance De la Rey's reputation
- Methuen's force collapsed in the face of De la Rey's sudden attack on 7 March 1902, and Methuen was captured

## Post-War Years and Later Life

After his release, Nicolaas van Rensburg returned to his farm Rietkuil, near Wolmaranstad. Some of his visions were then recorded by reverend Dr Rossouw. Van Rensburg's daughter Anna Badenhorst also recorded a set of visions up to his death at age 61. The latter set is considered to be difficult to interpret and not very coherent.

**World War I Rebellion (1914):**
When the Union of South Africa came out in support of the Allied Powers in World War I, van Rensburg allied with the rebels. The rebellion received a fatal blow even before it started, when the influential general Koos de la Rey was killed at a police road block on 15 September 1914.

**Final Years:**
- Continued to have visions until his death in 1926
- Lived on his farm Rietkuil near Wolmaranstad
- His visions during this period were recorded by his daughter Anna Badenhorst
- These later visions are considered more difficult to interpret and less coherent than his earlier ones

## Van Rensburg's Vision Interpretation Methodology

### Source: South African Military History Society Journal (1971)

#### Key Finding: Van Rensburg Did NOT Interpret His Own Visions

**Critical Statement:** "The 'Siener' saw many visions most of which were concerned with bulls or buck, and, though he was not always right, he was often exceedingly accurate. **The 'Siener' never interpreted his own visions, but left others to do so.** Thus, when things did not turn out as they should have done, or might have done, the fault was not his, but that of the interpreter."

**Additional Confirmation:** "Moreover, and this was perhaps the secret of his continued success, his visions were always symbolic and mysterious; possessing an adaptability of character that was truly Delphic. Indeed his hearers were compelled to put their own interpretation upon his visions. **The 'Siener' seldom pretended to understand or explain them himself.**"

#### Physical Description and Characteristics:

**Appearance (1899):** "In appearance the 'Siener' was small in stature, with a bushy black beard; he was then about 37 years old and was a farmer. What was unusual about him were his eyes, which immediately attracted attention, for they were a focal point, almost three dimensional. His colleagues both feared and respected him, and for these reasons, conferred the title of 'Oom' on him, usually reserved as a sign of respect for a much older man."

#### Specific Vision Examples with Details:

##### 1. Vision of Methuen's Convoy (Battle of Tweebosch)

**Van Rensburg's Vision:** "I see a red bull coming from the direction of Vryburg. His horns are pointing forwards. He is eager to fight. He is brave and strong but, when he arrives at Barberspan, his horns hang lower. His determination is failing and he begins to feel discouraged. But it will go even worse with him because, when he arrives at the Harts River, he will be completely dehorned. He will be unable to butt. He must be disarmed then."

**Van Rensburg's Interpretation:** "The 'Siener' also saw the Boers walking about between the British guns and wagons. He was thus convinced that the Boers would beat the British force."

**Historical Outcome:** General De la Rey was accustomed to ask the "Siener" for his prognosis before any battle, and it is probable that the sight of the "Siener" influenced the outcome of this particular battle. The battle of Tweebosch followed. General De la Rey's veterans charged the convoy and British infantry, firing from the saddle. Lord Methuen's force was overwhelmed and he was wounded. In spite of opposition from his men, De la Rey sent Methuen to hospital at Klerksdorp.

##### 2. Vision of General de la Rey's Death

**Van Rensburg's Vision:** "The 'Siener' had beheld the number 15 on a dark cloud from which blood issued, and then General De la Rey returning home without his hat."

**Van Rensburg's Interpretation:** "What these things portended, Van Rensburg could not say. He believed that they signified some high honour for the General."

**Historical Outcome:** "Both she [Polly, the General's daughter] and her father accepted that the vision of the General, without his hat, implied that he would be dead, for this was the 'Siener's' usual interpretation of such events. General de la Rey was to die on the 15th September, 1914, and incidentally 1914 also adds up to 15."

##### 3. Vision of General Botha's Death

**Van Rensburg's Vision:** "One of the 'Siener's' last recorded visions was a detailed description of General Botha's death and funeral when there was no reason to suspect that the General's end was near."

**Historical Context:** "The 'Siener' died on his farm Rietkuil, district Wolmaranstad, on the 11th March, 1926."
