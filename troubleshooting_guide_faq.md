## SECTION 1: SEED AND GERMINATION PROBLEMS

### PROBLEM 1: Seeds Not Sprouting After 3+ Days

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Seeds planted 3+ days ago with no green shoots visible
- Seeds look the same as when you planted them
- Some seeds may look swollen but no green growth
- You're worried you did something wrong

**Most likely causes:**
- Seeds are too old or stored improperly
- Water temperature is too cold (below 15°C)
- Seeds are planted too deep in the growing medium
- Growing area is too cold (below 18°C)
- Seeds are getting too much or too little water

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**If seeds are old or bad quality:**
1. Test your seeds: Take 10 seeds, place on wet paper towel, keep warm for 24 hours
2. If less than 8 out of 10 sprout, your seeds are bad
3. Buy fresh seeds from Seeds for Africa (021 201 1118)
4. Store new seeds in cool, dry place in sealed containers

**If temperature is too cold:**
1. Move growing area to warmer location (18-24°C is ideal)
2. Use a thermometer to check actual temperature
3. In winter, consider small heater near growing area
4. Cover trays with clear plastic to create mini greenhouse effect

**If seeds planted too deep:**
1. Gently brush away excess growing medium
2. Seeds should be barely covered - you should almost see them
3. For next planting: sprinkle seeds on surface, then lightly dust with growing medium

**If watering is wrong:**
1. Growing medium should feel like wrung-out sponge
2. If too dry: mist gently with spray bottle
3. If too wet: improve drainage, reduce watering frequency
4. Check that water is reaching all trays evenly

**Prevention for next time:**
- Always test new seed batches before large plantings
- Keep growing area at consistent 20-22°C
- Plant seeds at correct depth (barely covered)
- Check moisture daily with finger test

### PROBLEM 2: Seeds Sprouting Unevenly

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Some areas of tray have lots of sprouts, others have none
- Sprouts are different heights across the same tray
- Some seeds sprouted day 2, others still not sprouted day 5
- Patchy, uneven growth pattern

**Most likely causes:**
- Seeds not spread evenly when planting
- Uneven watering across the tray
- Some areas getting more light than others
- Growing medium not level in tray
- Some seeds are older/lower quality than others

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**For current crop:**
1. Identify areas with no sprouting
2. Gently add more seeds to empty areas
3. Ensure even watering by rotating trays daily
4. Move trays to ensure all get equal light

**For better planting technique:**
1. Measure seeds carefully (use measuring spoons)
2. Spread seeds by hand, not pouring from container
3. Shake tray gently to distribute seeds evenly
4. Check that growing medium is level before planting

**For watering improvements:**
1. Use spray bottle instead of pouring water
2. Spray in circular pattern to cover whole tray
3. Check that pump system reaches all areas equally
4. Rotate tray positions daily

**Prevention for next time:**
- Take time to spread seeds evenly by hand
- Level growing medium before planting
- Use consistent watering technique
- Rotate tray positions regularly

### PROBLEM 3: Seeds Going Moldy Instead of Sprouting

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- White, fuzzy growth on seeds (not green shoots)
- Bad smell from trays
- Seeds look slimy or rotting
- Growing medium smells sour or bad

**Most likely causes:**
- Too much water/poor drainage
- Poor air circulation around trays
- Contaminated growing medium or containers
- Seeds planted too thickly
- Temperature too high with high humidity

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Immediate action:**
1. Remove moldy trays immediately
2. Don't try to save moldy crops - throw them away
3. Clean containers with bleach solution (1:10 ratio)
4. Let containers dry completely before reusing

**Fix growing conditions:**
1. Reduce watering frequency
2. Improve drainage - ensure water doesn't pool
3. Increase air circulation with small fan
4. Reduce humidity by removing covers sooner
5. Space seeds less densely

**Clean everything:**
1. Wash all containers with bleach solution
2. Replace growing medium completely
3. Clean water tanks and change nutrient solution
4. Disinfect scissors and tools

**Prevention for next time:**
- Don't overwater - growing medium should be moist, not soggy
- Ensure good drainage in all trays
- Don't plant seeds too thickly
- Remove covers as soon as sprouting begins
- Keep growing area clean and well-ventilated

## SECTION 2: GROWTH AND DEVELOPMENT PROBLEMS

### PROBLEM 4: Microgreens Growing Too Slowly

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Sprouts are much shorter than expected for their age
- Growth seems to have stopped after initial sprouting
- Plants look weak and spindly
- Taking much longer than expected to reach harvest size

**Most likely causes:**
- Not enough light
- Nutrient solution too weak or wrong pH
- Temperature too low
- Poor air circulation
- Overcrowding

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Improve lighting:**
1. Move trays to brighter location
2. If using artificial lights, bring them closer (30-40cm from plants)
3. Increase daily light hours to 14-16 hours
4. Clean any dirty windows blocking natural light

**Fix nutrient solution:**
1. Test pH - should be 5.5-6.5
2. If pH wrong, adjust with pH Up or pH Down solutions
3. Check nutrient concentration - follow package instructions exactly
4. Change nutrient solution if it's more than 2 weeks old

**Improve temperature:**
1. Check temperature with thermometer - should be 18-24°C
2. Move growing area to warmer location if needed
3. Use small heater in winter
4. Avoid cold drafts from windows or doors

**Increase air circulation:**
1. Add small fan to move air gently
2. Don't point fan directly at plants
3. Ensure growing area isn't stuffy or enclosed

**Reduce overcrowding:**
1. For current crop: thin out weakest plants
2. For next planting: use fewer seeds per tray
3. Give plants room to grow

**Prevention for next time:**
- Provide adequate lighting from day 1
- Maintain proper pH and nutrients
- Keep consistent temperature
- Don't overcrowd plants

### PROBLEM 5: Microgreens Turning Yellow

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Green leaves turning pale yellow
- Plants look weak and unhealthy
- Yellow color starts at bottom and moves up
- Growth slowing down or stopping

**Most likely causes:**
- Not enough light (most common cause)
- Nutrient deficiency
- Overwatering
- Plants getting too old (past harvest time)

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**If caused by poor lighting:**
1. Move to brighter location immediately
2. Add artificial lighting if natural light insufficient
3. Ensure 12-16 hours of light daily
4. Remove any obstacles blocking light

**If caused by nutrient problems:**
1. Test nutrient solution strength
2. Mix fresh nutrient solution following package directions exactly
3. Check pH and adjust to 5.5-6.5
4. Ensure nutrients are reaching all plants

**If caused by overwatering:**
1. Reduce watering frequency
2. Improve drainage
3. Let growing medium dry slightly between waterings
4. Check that water isn't pooling in trays

**If plants are too old:**
1. Harvest immediately even if smaller than ideal
2. Don't let microgreens get too mature
3. Plan harvest timing better for next crop

**Prevention for next time:**
- Provide adequate lighting from sprouting
- Maintain proper nutrient levels
- Don't overwater
- Harvest at optimal timing

### PROBLEM 6: Microgreens Growing Too Tall and Spindly

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Plants are very tall but thin and weak
- Stems are pale or white instead of green
- Plants fall over easily
- Leaves are small compared to stem length

**Most likely causes:**
- Not enough light (plants stretching toward light)
- Kept covered too long after sprouting
- Temperature too high
- Overcrowding

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Improve lighting immediately:**
1. Move to much brighter location
2. Add artificial lighting
3. Bring lights closer to plants
4. Increase daily light hours

**Remove covers:**
1. Take off any newspaper or covers immediately
2. Don't cover plants once green shoots appear
3. Let plants get direct light

**Reduce temperature:**
1. Move to cooler location (ideal 18-22°C)
2. Improve ventilation
3. Avoid hot, stuffy areas

**For next crop:**
1. Remove covers as soon as sprouting begins
2. Provide bright light from day 1
3. Don't overcrowd seeds
4. Maintain proper temperature

**Can you save current crop:**
- If stems are very weak, harvest early for baby microgreens
- Use immediately as they won't store well
- Focus on preventing this in next crop

### PROBLEM 7: Microgreens Have Poor Flavor

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Microgreens taste bitter or unpleasant
- Flavor is much stronger/harsher than expected
- Customers complaining about taste
- You don't want to eat your own product

**Most likely causes:**
- Harvesting too late (plants too mature)
- Stress from poor growing conditions
- Wrong varieties for your taste preference
- Poor post-harvest handling

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Harvest timing:**
1. Harvest earlier - when first true leaves just appear
2. Don't wait for plants to get large
3. Taste test daily once plants reach minimum size
4. Different varieties ready at different times

**Reduce plant stress:**
1. Maintain consistent watering
2. Avoid temperature extremes
3. Provide adequate but not excessive light
4. Ensure good air circulation

**Improve post-harvest handling:**
1. Harvest in early morning when plants are crisp
2. Rinse gently in cold water immediately
3. Dry thoroughly but gently
4. Store in refrigerator immediately

**For future crops:**
1. Try different varieties to find preferred flavors
2. Keep detailed notes on harvest timing vs flavor
3. Maintain optimal growing conditions
4. Handle harvested product carefully

## SECTION 3: SYSTEM AND EQUIPMENT PROBLEMS

### PROBLEM 8: Water Not Reaching All Pipes

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Some pipes getting lots of water, others getting little or none
- Uneven plant growth across different pipes
- Some trays staying dry while others are soaked
- Water pressure seems low

**Most likely causes:**
- Blocked or kinked tubing
- Pump not powerful enough
- Uneven distribution system
- Air bubbles in lines

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Check tubing:**
1. Inspect all tubing for kinks, bends, or blockages
2. Straighten any kinked sections
3. Clear blockages with wire or by flushing with water
4. Replace damaged tubing sections

**Check pump capacity:**
1. Verify pump is rated for your system size
2. Clean pump intake filter
3. Check that pump is fully submerged
4. Consider upgrading to more powerful pump if needed

**Improve distribution:**
1. Install more T-joints to split water flow evenly
2. Use same diameter tubing throughout system
3. Keep tubing runs as short as possible
4. Install flow control valves to balance water distribution

**Remove air bubbles:**
1. Run system for 30 minutes to purge air
2. Tap tubing to release trapped air bubbles
3. Ensure all connections are tight
4. Prime pump properly before starting

**Prevention:**
- Regular system maintenance and cleaning
- Check tubing monthly for damage
- Keep spare tubing and fittings on hand
- Monitor water distribution daily

### PROBLEM 9: pH Levels Keep Changing

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- pH reading different every day
- Plants showing signs of nutrient problems
- pH drifts up or down despite adjustments
- Inconsistent plant growth

**Most likely causes:**
- Nutrient solution too old
- pH meter needs calibration
- Contamination in water system
- Natural pH drift as plants consume nutrients

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Replace nutrient solution:**
1. Drain and clean both tanks completely
2. Mix fresh nutrient solution
3. Test pH of fresh solution
4. Adjust to 5.5-6.5 before adding to system

**Calibrate pH meter:**
1. Use pH calibration solutions (pH 4.0 and 7.0)
2. Follow meter instructions exactly
3. If using test strips, buy fresh ones
4. Store pH meter properly between uses

**Clean system:**
1. Flush entire system with clean water
2. Clean tanks with mild bleach solution
3. Rinse thoroughly before adding nutrients
4. Replace any old or contaminated water

**Monitor and adjust:**
1. Check pH daily at same time
2. Make small adjustments (0.2-0.3 pH units at a time)
3. Wait 30 minutes after adjustments before retesting
4. Keep log of pH readings and adjustments

**Prevention:**
- Change nutrient solution every 2 weeks
- Keep system clean
- Calibrate pH meter monthly
- Monitor pH daily

### PROBLEM 10: Pumps Making Too Much Noise

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Loud humming, grinding, or rattling sounds
- Noise disturbing neighbors or household
- Pumps vibrating excessively
- Sound getting worse over time

**Most likely causes:**
- Pump not fully submerged
- Debris in pump impeller
- Pump mounted on hard surface causing vibration
- Pump wearing out or damaged

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Check water level:**
1. Ensure pump is completely underwater
2. Add water to tank if level too low
3. Pump should be at least 5cm below water surface
4. Check that pump intake isn't blocked

**Clean pump:**
1. Turn off and disconnect pump
2. Remove and clean pump impeller
3. Clear any debris from pump housing
4. Reassemble carefully

**Reduce vibration:**
1. Place pump on rubber mat or foam pad
2. Don't let pump touch tank walls
3. Secure tubing so it doesn't vibrate
4. Check that pump is sitting level

**If pump is damaged:**
1. Check warranty - may be replaceable
2. Consider upgrading to quieter pump model
3. Look for pumps specifically designed for quiet operation
4. Install pump in separate, insulated box

**Prevention:**
- Keep pumps fully submerged
- Clean pumps monthly
- Use vibration dampening materials
- Buy quality pumps with good warranties

### PROBLEM 11: Water Leaking from System

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Water puddles around growing area
- Water level dropping faster than expected
- Wet spots on floor or surfaces
- System losing pressure

**Most likely causes:**
- Loose connections in tubing
- Cracked or damaged tubing
- Overfilled tanks
- Damaged tank or container

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Find the leak:**
1. Run system and watch for dripping water
2. Check all tubing connections
3. Inspect tubing for cracks or holes
4. Look for water stains showing leak location

**Fix tubing problems:**
1. Tighten loose connections
2. Replace damaged tubing sections
3. Use proper fittings for secure connections
4. Wrap connections with waterproof tape if needed

**Fix tank problems:**
1. Check tank for cracks
2. Repair small cracks with waterproof sealant
3. Replace damaged tanks
4. Don't overfill tanks - leave space for water circulation

**Prevent future leaks:**
1. Use quality tubing and fittings
2. Don't overtighten connections (can crack fittings)
3. Protect tubing from damage
4. Regular inspection of all connections

## SECTION 4: HARVEST AND POST-HARVEST PROBLEMS

### PROBLEM 12: Microgreens Wilting After Harvest

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Fresh-cut microgreens becoming limp within hours
- Leaves drooping and losing crispness
- Product looking poor quality quickly
- Customers complaining about freshness

**Most likely causes:**
- Harvesting during hot part of day
- Not cooling product quickly enough
- Poor handling during harvest
- Inadequate packaging

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Improve harvest timing:**
1. Harvest early morning (6-8 AM) when plants are crisp
2. Avoid harvesting during hot afternoon
3. Harvest when plants are fully hydrated
4. Work quickly to minimize time out of refrigeration

**Cool immediately:**
1. Rinse harvested microgreens in cold water
2. Dry gently but thoroughly
3. Package and refrigerate within 30 minutes
4. Use insulated containers for transport

**Handle gently:**
1. Use sharp, clean scissors
2. Don't squeeze or compress microgreens
3. Work in small batches
4. Keep harvested product in shade

**Improve packaging:**
1. Use breathable containers (not airtight)
2. Include small amount of moisture but not too much
3. Don't pack too tightly
4. Label with harvest date

**Prevention:**
- Always harvest in cool morning hours
- Have refrigeration ready before harvesting
- Handle product gently throughout process
- Use proper packaging materials

### PROBLEM 13: Short Shelf Life

**Symptoms you'll see:**
- Microgreens going bad within 2-3 days
- Yellowing or browning quickly
- Bad smell developing
- Customers returning product

**Most likely causes:**
- Harvesting too late (overmatured plants)
- Poor post-harvest handling
- Incorrect storage temperature
- Contamination during processing

**EXACT SOLUTIONS:**

**Harvest at right time:**
1. Harvest when first true leaves just appear
2. Don't wait for plants to get large
3. Younger plants have longer shelf life
4. Different varieties ready at different times

**Improve handling:**
1. Use clean, sanitized tools and containers
2. Wash hands thoroughly before handling
3. Rinse product in clean, cold water
4. Dry completely but gently

**Optimize storage:**
1. Store at 2-4°C (refrigerator temperature)
2. Use breathable packaging
3. Don't store in airtight containers
4. Keep humidity moderate (not too dry, not too wet)

**Prevent contamination:**
1. Clean all surfaces and tools with sanitizer
2. Use clean water for rinsing
3. Don't cross-contaminate between batches
4. Store different varieties separately

**Prevention:**
- Harvest at optimal timing
- Maintain strict cleanliness
- Store at proper temperature
- Use appropriate packaging

## SECTION 6: FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

### GENERAL QUESTIONS

**Q: How long does it really take to start making money?**
A: Following this guide exactly, you should make your first sale by Day 17 and be earning R1000+ weekly by Day 30. Significant income (R5000+ weekly) typically comes by Day 60-90.

**Q: What if I can't find all the suppliers you mentioned?**
A: The suppliers listed are the best options, but alternatives exist. Call Seeds for Africa first (021 201 1118) - they can often recommend other suppliers. Builders Warehouse and Makro are everywhere and have basic equipment.

**Q: How much money do I really need to start?**
A: Following this guide, you need R6,500-10,000 for equipment and supplies. This is much less than other business opportunities and you'll start earning money within 3 weeks.

**Q: What if my family thinks this is a crazy idea?**
A: Show them this guide and the financial projections. Start small, prove it works, then expand. Many successful businesses started with family skepticism. Your results will speak for themselves.

**Q: Can I do this if I work a full-time job?**
A: Yes! The daily routine takes 30-60 minutes. You can harvest and deliver on weekends. Many successful growers started part-time.

### TECHNICAL QUESTIONS

**Q: What if my pH meter breaks?**
A: Use pH test strips as backup (available at pool shops). For emergency, add 1 teaspoon lemon juice per 10 liters to lower pH, or 1/2 teaspoon baking soda per 10 liters to raise pH.

**Q: Can I use tap water instead of filtered water?**
A: Cape Town tap water is generally fine for microgreens. Let it sit 24 hours before use to let chlorine evaporate. If you have very hard water, consider a simple filter.

**Q: What if my pumps break?**
A: Keep spare pumps or have backup plan. You can water manually for a few days if needed. Most pumps come with warranties - keep receipts.

**Q: How do I know if my nutrients are still good?**
A: Nutrients last 1-2 years if stored properly. If solution smells bad or has strange color, replace it. When in doubt, mix fresh solution.

### BUSINESS QUESTIONS

**Q: Do I need business registration and licenses?**
A: For small-scale operations, you can start informally. As you grow, consider registering as a business for tax benefits and credibility. Check with local municipality about any permits needed.

**Q: What about insurance?**
A: Start with basic household insurance covering your equipment. As you grow, consider business insurance. Most important is product liability if selling to restaurants.

**Q: How do I handle taxes?**
A: Keep detailed records of all income and expenses. Consult with accountant once earning significant income. Many business expenses are tax-deductible.

**Q: What if customers don't pay?**
A: Start with cash-on-delivery. For regular customers, consider 7-day payment terms. Don't extend credit until you know customers well.

### SEASONAL QUESTIONS

**Q: Can I grow microgreens in Cape Town winter?**
A: Yes! Indoor growing means year-round production. You may need supplemental heating and lighting in winter. Some varieties actually prefer cooler conditions.

**Q: What about load shedding affecting my pumps?**
A: Install timer systems that restart automatically after power returns. Consider small backup battery system for critical pumps. Manual watering can work for short outages.

**Q: How does Cape Town's water restrictions affect me?**
A: Hydroponic systems use 90% less water than traditional farming. Your water usage will be minimal. Collect and reuse water where possible.

### SCALING QUESTIONS

**Q: When should I expand my system?**
A: Expand when you're consistently selling 80%+ of your production and have waiting customers. Usually around Day 45-60 if following this guide.

**Q: How big can this business get?**
A: Very big! Some microgreens operations generate R50,000+ monthly. Start small, prove the concept, then scale systematically.

**Q: Should I hire employees?**
A: Not initially. You can handle significant production alone. Consider part-time help when reaching R15,000+ monthly sales.

**Q: What about competition?**
A: Cape Town market is large enough for many growers. Focus on quality, service, and relationships rather than just price competition.
